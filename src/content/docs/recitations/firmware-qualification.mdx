Recitation 7 covers **testing**, the material from the lecture of the
week before. It follows [Dashboard Publication Check](/assignments/dashboard-publication-tests/), and the problems are
fresh variations of the same skeletons that assignment had you practice.

No Rubber Duck gadget ships until its safety checks are qualified, and qualifying a check means more than running it once and watching it look right. Gary hands you the qualification tickets for the spring line and expects evidence, not optimism.

## What this session covers

- Deriving test cases from a specification rather than from the code in front of you
- Choosing cases that would catch a version of the code that got the rule wrong
- Saying what a given test case actually proves, and what it leaves untested
- Reading a program and locating the behaviour that fails its specification

## How the session runs

The session runs for 110 minutes, in person, on paper. You get a printed handout
when you arrive and you write your answers on it; there is no machine, no
network, and nothing to submit online. You may answer in Python or in Rust, and a
correct answer in either earns full marks.

The handout is **not published here or anywhere else**. That is deliberate: the
problems are a graded assessment, and one you could read in advance would not
measure anything. What is published is this page, so you know what to prepare.

## How to prepare

Do the assignment above honestly, by yourself. The recitation asks the same kind
of question at the same level of difficulty, with different values and a
different story, and the only support removed is the scaffolding: no hints, no
worked sub-steps, and no tools. A student who did the practice should walk in
expecting to succeed.
