An activity is the hands-on companion to a lecture. Where the lecture note explains a concept for you to read before class, the activity is where you first *use* that concept, in class, with the instructor and your classmates working through short problems together. Each activity pairs with exactly one lecture and gives you a guided first attempt at applying it, one small step harder than the worked examples in the reading.

## In Class, or on Your Own

In class, the instructor poses each short problem, you attempt it, and then it is worked out together on the spot. Every activity is built to also serve a student who missed that day: each problem's answer sits in a panel you can open after you have tried the problem yourself, so you get the same resolution the class did. Attempt first, then open the panel and check. One page, whether you are in the room or catching up later.

Like the lectures, the activities are **dual-language**: where a problem looks different in Python and Rust, it shows both side by side. They carry no story and no grade; they exist only to give you a first, low-pressure repetition before the take-home assignment and the recitation ask you to do it on your own.
