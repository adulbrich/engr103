The course wears two story lines, one for take-home practice and one for the supervised tiers, and the story is always skippable and never changes the technical task.

## The Willamette Resource Office (assignments)

In the assignments you are a junior engineer on the software team of the **Willamette Resource Office (WRO)**, a fictional public agency that manages sustainability work across the Pacific Northwest. There is no plot: each assignment is its own piece of the office's work, not part of a story. Missing a week never puts you behind. The recurring character is AURA, the office's AI assistant, bought from an outside vendor. AURA is helpful, tireless, and confidently wrong at convenient moments. On each assignment AURA offers a claim: a confident, plausible statement about how to solve the problem, stated in words, never as a block of code. The claim is sometimes wrong. Your job is to judge the claim and reject it if it does not hold up, using your own test cases to settle the question. AURA is wrong about numbers and methods only, never about a policy question. The office rule is simple: no AURA analysis is published unverified. You always check AURA's claim before it counts. Placing AURA in the assignments is deliberate: they are the one tier where you may use AI, so a confidently wrong AI you must judge is the right training partner.

## Rubber Duck Robotics (recitations)

The graded recitations are set at Rubber Duck Robotics, a mediocre but lovable novelty-gadget company that makes things like self-stirring mugs and motivational bathtub ducks. In the room you are the new firmware intern. Each recitation arrives as a ticket from Gary, the senior engineer, who is enthusiastic, overcommitted, and reliably wrong about one thing. Gary usually sends a helper function that looks convenient but is subtly wrong. Copying it without checking costs you a visible test, so read what Gary gives you before you trust it.

## The problem families and their two faces

Each problem family is one concept skeleton worn as a different costume in each tier. The same skeleton shows up as a Willamette Resource Office task in the assignments and as a Rubber Duck Robotics ticket in the recitations; the exams wear no story at all. Every problem can be solved while ignoring the setting.

| Family | Rubber Duck face (recitations) | Willamette Resource Office face (assignments) | Core topics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arithmetic with units and time** | Duck-race countdown timers, novelty-mug fill volumes | Vehicle lifecycle payback, appliance energy, concrete mix targets | Expressions, types, variables, functions |
| **Decision tables and interlocks** | Hot-tub controller: temperature, occupancy, and timer cutoff rules | Burn-window clearance, discharge permits, dashboard publication gates | Booleans, conditionals, decision tables |
| **Loops over state and grids** | Warehouse duckbot fetching parts, ASCII duck-parade rendering | Solar array degradation, multi-year inventory accumulation | Loops, loop patterns, nested loops, functions |
| **Encode, decode, clean a sequence** | Garbled customer order codes, novelty-pager messages | Compact field-logger telemetry codes, station identifiers | Strings, lists/vectors, memory model, sharing |
| **Validate and summarize a noisy stream** | The smart bathtub's water-level sensor returns nonsense | Low-cost sensor drift against a reference monitor, tolerance alarms | Errors, input validation, floating point, RNG noise |
| **Key-value lookup and counting** | Duck SKU inventory after a warehouse mix-up | Station manifests, waste stream categories (week-10 extra) | Dictionaries/hashmaps, choosing structures |
