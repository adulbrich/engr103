The assignments are your take-home practice: the place to work through a problem family with any tool you like, including AI, before a proctored recitation tests the same skeleton without them. They are low-stakes on purpose. Together they are worth a small share of your grade, they are graded on completion rather than perfection. The point is not the points. The point is that you arrive at each recitation having already built the thing it will ask you to build again.

Because they are the tier where AI is allowed, they are also the tier that trains you to **judge** code, not just produce it. That is where HAB comes in.

## Mission Ares

You are a member of the flight software crew of **Mission Ares**, the first crewed habitat on Mars. Across the term the assignments follow one mission, one episode at a time: preparing the vehicle, the launch, the burn that sends you to Mars, the long transit, the first days on the surface, and finally the Sol 100 status report that sums up everything the software has to get right. Each assignment opens with a mission beat that sets the scene and hands you a job.

Your crewmate on every assignment is **HAB**, the habitat's onboard AI. HAB is helpful, tireless, and, at exactly the wrong moment, confidently wrong. In most assignments HAB drafts a piece of code and asks you to sign off on it. Sometimes it is fine. Sometimes it has a quiet defect: a precedence slip, an off-by-one, a value that leaks where it should have been copied. Your job is to work out which, and to reject and rewrite what does not hold up. HAB is on the assignment tier deliberately: this is the one tier where you may use AI, so learning to catch a confident machine when it is wrong is part of the training, not a distraction from it.

The story is a spine, not a cage. Every requirement is numbered and testable, and you can earn full marks with the plot skipped entirely. The mission never hides the technical ask.

## How an Assignment Works

Each regular assignment has the same parts, in this order:

- **A problem set**, the main work, checked by an automatic grader. Before the Functions lecture, you complete a provided program and the grader compares its printed output to what is expected. From the Functions lecture on, you write named functions against a provided harness, and the grader calls your functions directly and checks what they return.
- **A paper practice**, one tracing or diagram exercise done by hand and photographed, in the same format the exams use. The worked solution is on the page, hidden until you open it, so you attempt it first and then check yourself.
- **A reflection**, two sentences on what you used help for, including whether and how you used AI.
- **An optional summit problem** on some assignments, a harder stretch worth no points, there for the challenge.

Every assignment is solvable using only the language features the course has taught by the week it is due, so you are never blocked by something you have not seen yet. The [Language Ladder](/reference/language-ladder/) lists what that is, as a recommendation you can lean on when you are unsure; you are always free to use a feature you already know. See the [Schedule](/overview/schedule/) for which recitation each assignment prepares you for, and read [Running and Submitting Your Code](/practicalities/starter-repo-and-check/) for the mechanics of the starter repository and how to submit.

## The Chapters

The assignments run in mission order. Start with the systems check, then follow the mission from the pad to Sol 100.

1. [Systems Checkout](/assignments/systems-checkout/): bring your tools online before the mission begins.
2. [Pre-Flight Check](/assignments/pre-flight-check/): read the instrument panel before the launch window opens.
3. [The Launch Window](/assignments/launch-window/): the countdown and fuel numbers for launch.
4. [The Injection Burn](/assignments/injection-burn-math/): the burn that sends the crew toward Mars.
5. [Transit Checks](/assignments/transit-checks/): the interlocks that keep the crew safe in transit.
6. [The Airlock and the Readings](/assignments/airlock-and-readings/): validating the readings at the airlock.
7. [Flight Qualification](/assignments/flight-qualification/): testing the software before it is trusted.
8. [The First Drive](/assignments/first-drive/): the rover's first drive across the surface.
9. [The Comms Window](/assignments/comms-window/): encoding and decoding messages home.
10. [The Sol 100 Report](/assignments/sol-100/): the capstone status report, one hundred sols in.
