An activity is the hands-on companion to a lecture. Where the lecture note explains a concept for you to read before class, the activity is where you first *use* that concept, in class, with the instructor and your classmates working through short problems together. Each activity pairs with exactly one lecture and gives you a guided first attempt at applying it, one small step harder than the worked examples in the reading.

## In Class, or on Your Own

In class, the instructor poses each short problem, you attempt it, and then it is worked out together on the spot. Every activity is built to also serve a student who missed that day: each problem's answer sits in a panel you can open after you have tried the problem yourself, so you get the same resolution the class did. Attempt first, then open the panel and check. One page, whether you are in the room or catching up later.

Like the lectures, the activities are **dual-language**: where a problem looks different in Python and Rust, it shows both side by side. They carry no story. They are graded for **completeness, not correctness**: you submit your Python or Rust file or files for the activity on Canvas, and you earn credit for doing the work, not for getting every answer right. They exist to give you a first, low-pressure repetition before the take-home assignment and the recitation ask you to do it on your own.

## The Activities

Week 1's two lectures are an introduction and have no activity; the activities begin with the third lecture and run one per lecture from there.

- [Expressions and Operators](/activities/expressions-and-operators/): evaluate mixed-operator expressions and integer versus float division.
- [Variables and State](/activities/variables-and-state/): trace how a variable's value changes as a program runs.
- [Functions](/activities/functions/): define, call, and trace small functions.
- [Scope and the Call Stack](/activities/scope-and-the-call-stack/): follow which names are visible and how calls return.
- [Booleans and Conditionals](/activities/booleans-and-conditionals/): build and check conditions with comparisons and `and`/`or`/`not`.
- [Decision Structures](/activities/decision-structures/): write multi-outcome decisions with `elif`/`else if` and `match`.
- [Errors, Input, and Validation](/activities/errors-input-and-validation/): parse input and validate a value before using it.
- [Testing](/activities/testing/): derive test cases from a specification.
- [Loops](/activities/loops/): write `while` and `for` loops that stop at the right time.
- [Loop Patterns](/activities/loop-patterns/): apply the accumulate, count, search, and validate patterns.
- [Strings and Characters](/activities/strings/): index, slice, and build text.
- [Collections I: Lists and Vectors](/activities/collections-i/): index, grow, and iterate a list.
- [The Memory Model: Aliasing and Ownership](/activities/the-memory-model/): draw how names share or own their values.
- [Sharing and Mutation](/activities/sharing-and-mutation/): pass a collection to a function and see when the caller changes.
- [Collections II: Dictionaries and Maps](/activities/collections-ii/): store and look up values by key.
- [Computing with Judgment](/activities/computing-with-judgment/): judge code you did not write.

Each activity names, at its end, the recitation problem family it feeds, so you can see how the day's practice connects to what will be assessed. See the [Schedule](/overview/schedule/) for the week-by-week map.
