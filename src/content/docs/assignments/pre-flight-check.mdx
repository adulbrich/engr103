Before the launch window opens, the crew runs a pre-flight check: bring up the instrument panel, confirm the manifest, and read the status word off the bus. This is your first coding assignment, and it is deliberately at the foundation of everything that follows. You have only just seen how a program runs and how values are stored, so each program here is nearly complete; you fill in the readings. HAB has offered one of them, so check it before it goes on the board.

## How This Assignment Works

You have not learned variables or functions yet, so each problem gives you a **complete, runnable program with marked `TODO` lines**. You replace each placeholder with a literal or an arithmetic expression that computes the reading described, and the automated grader runs your program and compares its printed output to the expected output shown below the problem. Solve each problem in Python or Rust; both are checked against the expected output for that language. Submit from the starter repository as described in [Running and Submitting Your Code](/practicalities/starter-repo-and-check/). The assignment has three parts: the three programs below, two paper reductions, and a two-sentence reflection.

Two rules hold for every program here, and both come straight from the [Data Representation and Memory](/lectures/data-representation/) lecture. First, an expression may not mix a whole number with a decimal number: Rust refuses to compile `3 + 2.5`, so any reading that needs a fraction must be written with **every** number as a float (for example `100.5`, not `100`). Second, to get a real fraction from division you must divide floats, because dividing two whole numbers is a separate rule you have not met yet.

## Paper Practice

Reduce each expression by hand, on paper, one operator at a time, and write the single value it comes out to. Photograph your steps and attach them. This part is graded on completion, not correctness, so reduce them yourself first, then open the reveal to check.

The first is all whole numbers and includes a binary literal and a set of parentheses. The second is all decimals and includes a division. Keep the two kinds of number separate, exactly as the programs above required.

```text title="Reduce these two expressions"
3 * 4 - 2 * (0b101 - 1) + 6

10.0 / 4.0 + 3.0 * 2.0 - 1.0
```

<Reveal label="Reveal the two reductions">
```text title="Reducing 3 * 4 - 2 * (0b101 - 1) + 6"
3 * 4 - 2 * (0b101 - 1) + 6
3 * 4 - 2 * (5 - 1) + 6       (0b101 is the whole number 5)
3 * 4 - 2 * 4 + 6            (the parentheses reduce first: 5 - 1 is 4)
12 - 8 + 6                   (both products form before the + and -: 3 * 4 is 12, 2 * 4 is 8)
10                          (left to right: 12 - 8 is 4, then 4 + 6 is 10)
```

```text title="Reducing 10.0 / 4.0 + 3.0 * 2.0 - 1.0"
10.0 / 4.0 + 3.0 * 2.0 - 1.0
2.5 + 6.0 - 1.0             (the / and the * run before the + and -: 10.0 / 4.0 is 2.5, 3.0 * 2.0 is 6.0)
7.5                        (left to right: 2.5 + 6.0 is 8.5, then 8.5 - 1.0 is 7.5)
```

Parentheses reduce first, then multiplication and division before addition and subtraction, and operators of equal standing run left to right. Because every number in the second expression is a float, the division gives a real fraction and both languages print `7.5`.
</Reveal>
